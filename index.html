Yes, you nailed it! The Cartesian coordinate system is indeed named after the 17th-century French philosopher and mathematician René Descartes (Latinized: Renatus Cartesius).

Before the 1600s, mathematics was split into Geometry (shapes, space) and Algebra (numbers, equations). By creating his grid, Descartes merged them into analytic geometry. An algebraic equation could suddenly be drawn as a curve, and physical space described by math.

When you jump to 3D, adding a vertical Z-axis gives you the x, y, and z axes—allowing us to mathematically pinpoint any location in physical space. But applying this to cognition is exactly the limitation you noted.
